## You must do before submitting
1. **Authors.** Replace "Anonymous Authors" with real names, affiliations, ORCIDs and the corresponding author (marked `TODO(authors)` in `main.tex`). Authors must be people who made the intellectual contribution and take responsibility; the AI system is not an author.
2. **AI disclosure.** Read the Acknowledgment and make it true for what you actually did. If the journal asks for the model name and version, add the Claude model version(s) used. Edit anything you did not do or did not check.
3. **Verify the content yourself.** You are responsible for every claim. At minimum re-read the Results and Limitations and run `python scripts/final_report.py && python scripts/round2_tables.py` once to confirm the numbers regenerate.
4. **Conflict of interest, funding, ethics.** The draft says "no competing interests", "public de-identified images only". Confirm these are true, add the funding statement, and check whether your institution wants an IRB exemption letter for use of public data.
5. **Plagiarism / similarity check.** Journals screen with a similarity tool. Run your institution's iThenticate (or equivalent) report on the final text before submission and review any match, especially in Related Work.
6. **Code link and anonymity.** The repository URL contains a GitHub username. If the journal is double-blind, replace it with an anonymized repository link; otherwise keep it.
7. **Choose the venue and check its indexing yourself.** Scopus indexes journals, not papers. Look the journal up at scopus.com/sources (confirm it is active, not discontinued) and check its current quartile (SJR or JCR) for the year you submit. Then read its author guidelines for page limit, template, reference style, data policy and AI policy; adjust (e.g. IEEE Access vs a society transaction).
8. **Cover letter.** State the main finding honestly (a frozen negative result plus conformal-calibration findings, not a new method that wins) and that the work was prepared with an AI system as disclosed.
9. **Copyright and forms.** IEEE collects the electronic copyright form after acceptance; have author details ready.

## Honest fit assessment
- The paper's contribution is an evaluation and a set of negative results on 924 images from one modality. That fits venues that publish careful empirical and reproducibility work better than venues that expect a new method with a clear accuracy gain. The strongest claims (conformal under-coverage, optimism of seen-site validation, replicated on two encoder families) are well supported; the accuracy-improvement direction (ensemble, GroupDRO) is **not significant** and the text says so.
- Likely reviewer objections: one dataset and modality; eight small sites; COVID-19 as a shortcut-prone benchmark; frozen (not fine-tuned) pretrained encoder; no multiplicity correction; possible overlap between the benchmark and the encoder's pretraining data (not checked). These are listed in the Limitations section.
- The strongest way to raise the paper's standing is more data: a second public multi-site dataset or more sites. This session could not reach such a dataset (the network allowlist blocked the hosts), so it was not done.
