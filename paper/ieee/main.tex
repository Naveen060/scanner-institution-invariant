\begin{abstract}
Diagnostic image models often perform poorly at hospitals absent from their training data. We tried to build a scanner- and institution-invariant pipeline from a class-conditional site adversary, augmentation based on measured per-site image statistics, and conformal prediction sets meant to hold at an unseen site. We tested it on an open chest-radiograph benchmark (924 images, 10 sources, COVID-19 versus other findings), holding out one site at a time. Four sites guided all design decisions; the other four were run once with settings frozen beforehand. The invariance ideas did not help: over eight held-out sites, none of eight alternative training methods beat plain empirical risk minimization (ERM) by more than noise (largest gain $+\TGroupdroDiffVsErm$ AUROC, GroupDRO; every 95\% interval included zero), and the result repeated with a frozen pretrained chest-radiograph encoder (largest gain $\RtGdroDiff$). The useful findings concern evaluation and uncertainty. Five acquisition statistics predicted the diagnosis on unseen sites (AUROC $\profLeakLogreg$). Validation on seen sites overstated held-out AUROC by $\OMeanSignedSeen$, and standard conformal sets covered only $\CPooledCovMean$ of cases at a $0.90$ target ($\RcPrePooledCov$ with the pretrained encoder). Leave-site-out CV+ calibration removed the worst shortfalls with sets about $26\%$ larger; all calibrations followed one coverage--size curve. Averaging a from-scratch CNN with the pretrained model gave the best mean and worst-site AUROC ($\RtEnsAll$ and $\RtEnsWorst$; $\RtEnsVsCnnAll$ [$\RtEnsVsCnnAllLo$, $\RtEnsVsCnnAllHi$] over one CNN), consistent across fold sets but not significant. The data are small and cover one modality; we make no claim about clinical COVID-19 detection.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{ diagnostic} model trained on pooled data from several hospitals can learn cues that identify the hospital instead of the disease~\cite{zech2018variable,degrave2021ai,geirhos2020shortcut}. It then looks good on validation data from the same hospitals and fails quietly at a new one. A common response is to make the model invariant to the scanner and institution through adversarial alignment, feature-statistics matching, harmonization, or augmentation~\cite{ganin2016domain,sun2016deep,zhang2020generalizing,johnson2007adjusting}.

We started this project to build such a recipe. It had three parts. First, a site adversary that works within each class, so that different disease prevalence across hospitals is not penalized~\cite{zhao2019learning}. Second, augmentation that re-renders an image as if it came from another \emph{observed} site, using statistics estimated from the training images without labels. Third, conformal prediction sets calibrated with an unseen site in mind. We then ran what we thought would be the deciding experiment: leave-one-site-out (LOSO) testing with a development/confirmation split and configurations frozen before the confirmation run.

The recipe did not work. Neither our method nor the baseline methods were reliably better than \erm{}. A natural objection is that the encoder was a small network trained from scratch, so we repeated the study on top of a frozen pretrained chest-radiograph encoder; the answer did not change. What survived are results about \emph{why} and about reporting uncertainty honestly for sites the model has not seen. We report both, since a frozen negative result is useful for anyone choosing among these methods on small multi-site data. Our contributions are modest, and we carefully mark novelty (our literature search was targeted, not systematic):
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item a frozen LOSO protocol on open data with a hierarchical paired bootstrap, a negative result for eight methods against \erm{} (three of them our own variants), the same negative result on a second, pretrained encoder family, and a controlled label-shift sweep;
  \item two label-free diagnostics of acquisition shortcuts, one measuring leakage of the label and one measuring how much a model relies on it;
  \item evidence on calibrating prediction sets for unseen sites, replicated on two encoder families and their ensemble: seen-site validation is optimistic, standard conformal sets under-cover, a site-fold CV+ rule avoids the worst cases, and simulated shifts do not reproduce the real effect;
  \item a pre-specified, suggestive but not significant, improvement from averaging a from-scratch CNN with a pretrained model.
\end{itemize}
We do not claim clinical COVID-19 detection performance; the benchmark is a shortcut-prone stress test.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Shortcuts in chest radiography.} Pneumonia and COVID-19 classifiers depend on site and dataset artifacts~\cite{zech2018variable,pooch2020can}. Masking the lungs barely changes classifier accuracy on public COVID-19 collections~\cite{maguolo2021critic}, classifiers can prefer shortcuts to signal~\cite{degrave2021ai}, and a large review found bias throughout this literature~\cite{roberts2021common,tartaglione2020unveiling}. Our diagnostics follow this line and add a held-out-site measurement.

\textbf{Invariance and domain generalization.} DANN, conditional adversaries, CORAL, GroupDRO, IRM, and MixStyle target shift~\cite{ganin2016domain,long2018conditional,sun2016deep,sagawa2020distributionally,arjovsky2019invariant,zhou2021domain}. DomainBed found that, with fair model selection, no method reliably beats \erm{}~\cite{gulrajani2021in}, and WILDS reported similar difficulty on real shifts~\cite{koh2021wilds}. Exact invariance is provably limited when label priors differ across domains~\cite{zhao2019learning}, and methods that handle joint conditional and label shift assume target data~\cite{liu2021adversarial,lipton2018detecting}. We studied the setting with no target data. Augmentation work (BigAug, stain augmentation, ComBat) is the closest relative of our site-profile augmentation~\cite{zhang2020generalizing,tellez2019quantifying,johnson2007adjusting}.

\textbf{Pretrained encoders and ensembles.} Pretraining on natural images gave limited gains for several medical imaging tasks~\cite{raghu2019transfusion}, while chest-radiograph encoders trained on many public collections are available as a library~\cite{cohen2022torchxrayvision}. Averaging independently trained networks is a simple way to improve accuracy and uncertainty estimates~\cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple}. We use both ideas as a second test of the same question and do not claim them as new.

\textbf{Conformal prediction under shift.} Split conformal prediction requires exchangeability~\cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}. Weighted and non-exchangeable versions handle known covariate shifts, drift, label shifts, and several sources~\cite{tibshirani2019conformal,barber2023conformal,podkopaev2021distribution,liu2024multisource}. For unseen domains, worst-case calibration over source domains or divergence balls exists~\cite{zou2024coverage}, and per-domain robust calibration is a baseline in set-valued domain generalization~\cite{tsibulsky2025set}; coverage failures of conformal sets under domain shift in medical imaging have been documented~\cite{mehrtens2025pitfalls}. Our worst-site mode is a variant of these and not new. CV+ comes from jackknife+~\cite{barber2021predictive}. We did not find an evaluation of CV+ with sites as folds on real multi-site medical images, but we did not search exhaustively.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Setting and training methods}
Data come from $K$ source sites that differ in image acquisition $P_k(X\mid Y)$ and prevalence $P_k(Y)$. Models are trained, selected, and calibrated on the source sites and then tested on one held-out site. The task is binary (COVID-19 vs.\ other). We denote $z$ as the encoder output and $s$ as the site index.

All methods share one encoder, optimizer, and budget. Baselines: \erm{}, GroupDRO (sites as groups), DANN (unconditional site adversary, weight $0.5$), and generic augmentation (intensity, gamma, noise, blur, and bias-field changes applied per sample). The variants we implemented:
\begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
  \item \emph{Class-conditional adversary.} One site discriminator per class behind a gradient-reversal layer, with each sample sent to the discriminator of its label: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}}=\mathbb{E}\,\mathrm{CE}(d_y(\mathrm{GRL}_\lambda(z)),s)$. A discriminator that only sees one class cannot exploit the prevalence differences. We pair it with per-class CORAL and site-prior reweighting of the task loss, $w_{s,y}=\pi(y)/\pi_s(y)$.
  \item \emph{Site-profile augmentation.} We measure five statistics per image (mean, contrast, noise floor, sharpness, and low-frequency bias), summarize them per training site without labels, and translate an image from its site toward another observed site by applying only the relative change. It is a measurement-driven proxy, not a scanner simulator.
  \item \emph{Profile adversarial invariance (PAI).} A gradient-reversal head attempts to regress the measured profile of the input from $z$.
  \item \emph{SIDL variants.} SIDL-cc: generic augmentation, class-conditional adversary, per-class CORAL, prior reweighting, and consistency loss between clean and augmented views. SIDL-phys: site-profile augmentation, unconditional adversary, pooled CORAL, consistency. SIDL-full: site-profile augmentation plus SIDL-cc components.
\end{itemize}
Mild settings (augmentation probability $0.3$, strength $0.5$, adversary weight $0.1$ for SIDL and PAI) were chosen on the development folds and frozen.

\subsection{Second encoder family: frozen pretrained features}
To test whether the conclusions depend on a small from-scratch CNN, we froze the DenseNet-121~\cite{huang2017densely} chest-radiograph encoder from the TorchXRayVision library~\cite{cohen2022torchxrayvision} (the ``all'' weights, trained on several public radiograph collections without COVID-19 labels), extracted its $1024$-dimensional embedding of each $224\times224$ image once, and trained only a small head: a linear layer to $256$ units with ReLU and dropout $0.2$, followed by a linear classifier ($30$ epochs, AdamW, learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size $64$, best source-validation AUROC). The head was trained with the same methods as above (\erm{}, prior reweighting, GroupDRO, DANN, class-conditional adversary with and without prior reweighting, CORAL, and the class-conditional recipe without image augmentation) at their default weights ($0.5$ adversary, $0.1$ CORAL), plus per-site feature centring. Centring subtracts a site mean from the features and uses the unlabeled target images for the held-out site, so it is transductive and not comparable with the other heads. Features are standardized with source-site statistics only. An \emph{ensemble} is the mean predicted probability of the models named; the combined model averages five CNNs and five pretrained heads (seeds $0$--$4$ each).

\subsection{Diagnostics}
\emph{Leak:} a logistic regression on the five profile numbers is trained on source sites and scored on the held-out site; an AUROC above $0.5$ means that acquisition statistics alone, without anatomy, predict the label. \emph{Reliance:} within each class, the Spearman correlation between a model's held-out $P(\text{COVID-19})$ and that profile-only score, averaged over classes; working within class means that the label cannot explain the correlation.

\subsection{Conformal calibration}
We use the score $1-p_\theta(y\mid x)$~\cite{sadinle2019least} and sets $\{y: 1-p_\theta(y\mid x)\le\hat q\}$. Three standard variants use the validation split of seen source sites: pooled, site-balanced (equal weight per site), and worst-site (largest per-site quantile among sites with at least $25$ images). For the leave-site-out (LSO) variants, we train an inner model for every source site $k$ on the other source sites and score \emph{all} images of site $k$ with it, giving scores $R_i$ that mimic behavior on an unseen site. LSO-split applies the pooled quantile of the $R_i$ to the deployed model. LSO-worst takes the largest per-site quantile. LSO-CV+ applies the CV+ rule with sites as folds: label $y$ enters the set for test image $x$ iff $\#\{i: R_i<1-p_{-k(i)}(y\mid x)\}<(1-\alpha)(n+1)$, where every inner model scores $x$. CV+ guarantees coverage for exchangeable \emph{points}~\cite{barber2021predictive}; here, the exchangeable units would be sites, we have only nine, and we have no theorem. Coverage is measured empirically. Besides coverage and mean set size at $\alpha=0.1$, we report the coverage-calibration error (mean $|\text{coverage}-(1-\alpha)|$ over nine values of $\alpha$ from $0.04$ to $0.30$), the fraction of (site, $\alpha$) cells more than five points under target, and set size interpolated at $90\%$ coverage.

\section{Benchmark and Protocol}
\subsection{Data}
We pooled three public GitHub collections of frontal chest radiographs~\cite{cohen2020covid,chung2020actualmed,chung2020figure1}. A site is the institution or country in the metadata. Small sites are merged into ``Other'' and images with no location form ``Unknown'', so these two groups mix institutions. The label is COVID-19 versus any other finding. Images are intensity-normalized, converted to grayscale, and area-downsampled to $64\times64$ for the CNN (and to $224\times224$ for the pretrained encoder). The benchmark has $\datasetTotal$ images from ten sources (Table~\ref{tab:datasets}), and prevalence varies a lot between them (SD $\priorSpreadSd$); therefore, label shift matters.

\subsection{Folds and freezing}
Eight sources were each held out once; Figure1 and Hannover-DE, which have only 5 and 3 negative images, were used for training only. We assigned four to development (Actualmed, Italy, China, and Spain) and four to confirmation (UK, Australia, Other, and Unknown) before looking at any confirmation result; the split was not randomized. Every design decision used development folds only. At the end of iteration 8, we froze nine CNN configurations and ran them once on the confirmation folds. Validation (a patient-level $15\%$ split) and checkpoint choice (best source-validation AUROC) never touched the held-out site. One exception is descriptive: the label-free diagnostics were computed once over all eight sites in the first iteration; they involved no tuned model and no choice among methods used them.

The pretrained family was added afterward (iterations 11--17 in the released log) under the same rules. Its head settings were examined on the development folds only (seven settings gave a mean AUROC of $0.737$--$0.743$, so the defaults were kept). Before its first confirmation run we wrote down the settings of all nine heads and two primary comparisons: (A) the combined ensemble against the five-CNN ensemble, and (B) the same ensemble against a single CNN. Then we ran the confirmation folds once. Two disclosures follow. The CNN confirmation results were already known when the pretrained family was designed, and three further CNN \erm{} seeds were run after the freeze with the unchanged configuration so that both families have five seeds.

\subsection{Models and statistics}
The CNN encoder has four conv--BatchNorm--ReLU--max-pool blocks (widths 16--64), trained from scratch with AdamW (learning rate $2\times10^{-3}$), batch size 64, 15 epochs, and two seeds for the nine-method comparison (five seeds for \erm{} in the family comparison), on two CPU cores (25--80\,s per run). The unit of generalization is the held-out site. For each method versus \erm{}, we take the paired AUROC difference (seed-averaged) per site, then bootstrap hierarchically: resample sites, then resample test images within each chosen site stratified by class, using the same images for both methods ($1500$ replicates; $2000$ for the family comparison). This is wider than a bootstrap over sites alone, which matters because some sites are tiny (Australia has 6 COVID-19 images; China has 5 negatives). For the label-shift and acquisition-shift experiments, we also build a semi-synthetic benchmark: images are assigned at random to five virtual sites with known transforms (gamma, contrast, blur, noise, and bias field) and controlled prior spread. Because the family comparison averages five CNN seeds, the CNN \erm{} mean over eight sites is $\RtCnnAll$ there and $\TErmAurocAll$ (two seeds) in Table~\ref{tab:main}.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Acquisition statistics leak labels}
Five numbers with no anatomy predicted the diagnosis on held-out sites with a mean AUROC of $\profLeakLogreg$ (gradient boosting: $\profLeakGbm$; Fig.~\ref{fig:diag}) and predicted the source site with an accuracy of $\siteAccProfile$ against $\siteAccChance$ for guessing the majority site. Re-measuring the leak on site-profile-augmented training images did not reduce it ($0.662\to0.660$); therefore, the label-correlated signal seems to live mostly \emph{within} sites (for example, portable versus fixed acquisition or patient severity) and not in site-level signatures that augmentation can randomize. Model scores track the same signal: within-class reliance is $\relErm$ for \erm{} and between $\relSiteProfileAug$ and $\relSidlFull$ for the other methods. Site-profile augmentation and PAI lowered it only slightly ($\relSiteProfileAug$, $\relProfileAdversaryPai$), and the two methods with a class-conditional adversary and prior reweighting had the highest values ($\relSidlCc$, $\relSidlFull$).

\subsection{No method significantly beats ERM}
\erm{} reached AUROC of $\TErmAurocDev$ on development folds and $\TErmAurocConf$ on confirmation folds (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Fig.~\ref{fig:methods}). Paired differences to \erm{} are small, and all intervals include zero: GroupDRO $+\TGroupdroDiffVsErm$ [$\TGroupdroLo$, $\TGroupdroHi$], SIDL-phys $+\TSidlPhysDiffVsErm$ [$\TSidlPhysLo$, $\TSidlPhysHi$], generic augmentation $\TGenericAugDiffVsErm$, PAI $\TProfileAdversaryPaiDiffVsErm$, DANN $\TDannDiffVsErm$, site-profile augmentation $\TSiteProfileAugDiffVsErm$, SIDL-cc $\TSidlCcDiffVsErm$, SIDL-full $\TSidlFullDiffVsErm$ [$\TSidlFullLo$, $\TSidlFullHi$]. Upper bounds rule out gains above about $0.055$ for every method. Orderings are unstable: method means correlate only $\rankCorrDevConf$ (Spearman) between development and confirmation folds. Site-profile augmentation tied \erm{} on development folds ($\TSiteProfileAugAurocDev$ vs.\ $\TErmAurocDev$) and fell below it on confirmation folds ($\TSiteProfileAugAurocConf$ vs.\ $\TErmAurocConf$); SIDL-phys moved the other way. GroupDRO also has higher balanced accuracy ($\TGroupdroBalAcc$ vs.\ $\TErmBalAcc$) and a better worst-site AUROC ($\TGroupdroWorstFold$ vs.\ $\TErmWorstFold$), but \erm{}'s worst site has only 6 positives, PAI does not share the gain, and balanced accuracy depends on an operating point that group reweighting shifts, so we treat both as exploratory. In an early iteration, whole-batch full-strength augmentation cost up to $0.083$ AUROC on development folds ($0.675$ vs.\ $0.758$); making it per-sample and mild removed the drop but never produced a gain.

\subsection{A frozen pretrained encoder gives the same answer}
Replacing the CNN by a frozen chest-radiograph encoder did not change the conclusion. Over eight sites the pretrained head is not clearly better than the CNN (AUROC $\RtPreAll$ vs.\ $\RtCnnAll$; difference $\RtPreVsCnnAll$ [$\RtPreVsCnnAllLo$, $\RtPreVsCnnAllHi$]). It is better at five sites (most clearly Australia and China) and worse at Actualmed, Italy, and UK (Fig.~\ref{fig:round2}a), and its mean ranks differently on the two fold sets ($\RtPreDev$ vs.\ $\RtCnnDev$ on development, $\RtPreConf$ vs.\ $\RtCnnConf$ on confirmation). None of the invariance heads beat \erm{} on the embedding (Table~\ref{tab:heads}). GroupDRO again had the largest gain, $\RtGdroDiff$ [$\RtGdroLo$, $\RtGdroHi$] ($\RtGdroDev$ on development folds, $\RtGdroConf$ on confirmation folds), with an interval that touches zero and the status of the best of nine uncorrected comparisons. The class-conditional adversary, CORAL, DANN, and prior reweighting were within $0.006$ of \erm{}; adversary plus prior reweighting was lower ($\RtCdannPDiff$), and site centring, which needs unlabeled target images, was clearly worse ($\RtCenterDiff$ [$\RtCenterLo$, $\RtCenterHi$]). Every upper bound was below $0.04$. The embedding still carries site information: a patient-grouped linear classifier names the source among ten with accuracy $\RdSiteAcc$ ($\RdSiteMaj$ for the majority source).

\subsection{Combining the two encoder families}
The two models fail at different sites. The CNN is weakest on Australia and Other, and the pretrained head on Actualmed and UK. Averaging five CNNs with five pretrained heads gave the best mean and the best worst-site AUROC (Table~\ref{tab:models}, Fig.~\ref{fig:round2}a). The two pre-specified comparisons were positive in direction on both fold sets and not significant: against the five-CNN ensemble $\RtEnsVsCnnEnsAll$ [$\RtEnsVsCnnEnsAllLo$, $\RtEnsVsCnnEnsAllHi$] (development $\RtEnsVsCnnEnsDev$, confirmation $\RtEnsVsCnnEnsConf$), and against a single CNN $\RtEnsVsCnnAll$ [$\RtEnsVsCnnAllLo$, $\RtEnsVsCnnAllHi$] (development $\RtEnsVsCnnDev$, confirmation $\RtEnsVsCnnConf$). \subsection{Conformal sets under-cover unseen sites}
Sets calibrated on seen-site validation data miss the target (Table~\ref{tab:conf}, Fig.~\ref{fig:conf}): mean coverage is $\CPooledCovMean$ (pooled) and $\CSiteBalancedCovMean$ (site-balanced) at the $0.90$ target, the worst site gets about $\CPooledCovMin$, and $\CPooledNUnder$ of $\CPooledN$ sites are more than five points short. The pattern shows up in both fold sets ($0.829$ on development, $0.843$ on confirmation for pooled). Worst-site calibration restores the mean ($\CWorstSiteCovMean$) but leaves two sites short (lowest $\CWorstSiteCovMin$, at ``Other''). LSO-CV+ reaches mean coverage $\CLsoCvplusCovMean$ and lowest site coverage $\CLsoCvplusCovMin$ with no site more than five points short, at mean set size $\CLsoCvplusSize$ against $\CPooledSize$ for pooled. It over-covers on most sites; LSO-split lands nearer the target on average ($\CLsoSplitCovMean$) but has a site at $\CLsoSplitCovMin$. Interpolated at $90\%$ coverage, set size is between $\CLsoSplitSizeAtCov$ and $\CSiteBalancedSizeAtCov$ for \emph{every} calibration (pooled $\CPooledSizeAtCov$, LSO-CV+ $\CLsoCvplusSizeAtCov$). Development folds had suggested that LSO-CV+ was about $4\%$ smaller at matched coverage; the confirmation folds did not repeat that. So the calibrations differ in operating point and not in efficiency, and LSO-CV+'s benefit is safety. Whether sets about $26\%$ larger are worth a six-point average shortfall (up to sixteen at the worst site) depends on the application. Two other ideas failed on development folds and were dropped: weighting calibration scores by a density ratio of the acquisition profile (coverage $0.834$ vs.\ $0.829$ for pooled), and correcting for estimated label shift~\cite{saerens2002adjusting,podkopaev2021distribution} (coverage $0.813$ with an EM prior, $0.845$ with the oracle prior; balanced accuracy got worse).

\emph{The same holds for the pretrained head and the ensemble} (Table~\ref{tab:confall}, Fig.~\ref{fig:round2}b). \subsection{Why: validation optimism}
For the CNN \erm{} model, we compared AUROC on seen-site validation data, the leave-site-out estimate (inner models on the sites they did not see), and the actual held-out AUROC (Fig.~\ref{fig:optim}). Over $\ONUnits$ site-seed units, seen-site validation overstates held-out AUROC by $\OMeanSignedSeen$ on average (absolute error $\OMaeSeen$); the leave-site-out estimate is nearly unbiased ($\OMeanSignedLso$; absolute error $\OMaeLso$). The pretrained head shows the same optimism ($\RdValAuc$ on seen-site validation vs.\ $\RdTestAuc$ on held-out sites, a gap of $\RdOptim$ over 40 units). Neither estimate ranks sites (Spearman with actual AUROC for the CNN: $\ORhoSeen$ and $\ORhoLso$): actual AUROC ranges from $0.56$ to $0.86$ while the leave-site-out estimate stays between $0.72$ and $0.75$, so it describes a typical unseen site and not a particular one. The per-site optimism gap and pooled-conformal coverage are rank-correlated in the expected direction ($\ORhoGapCov$ over eight sites), consistent with optimism driving under-coverage but not significant at this sample size. Averaging the inner models (a free by-product) gives AUROC $\ensAurocEns$ vs.\ $\ensAurocFull$ for the single model ($\ensDAuroc$ [$\ensDAurocLo$, $\ensDAurocHi$]), no significant gain.

\subsection{Simulated shifts are poor stand-ins}
On the semi-synthetic benchmark with three acquisition-severity levels, the seen-site pooled calibration already covers $0.896$--$0.922$ at the $0.90$ target, unlike the $\CPooledCovMean$ on real sites. The LSO variants are no better, and LSO-split under-covers at the highest severity ($0.834$, lowest site $0.688$). Therefore, the real under-coverage probably comes from differences between sites that our simulation does not contain (patient mix, case selection, labeling, and image provenance). A calibration method that was checked only on simulated acquisition shifts would have looked fine.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\subsection{What we think the evidence supports}
On this benchmark, augmentation, site adversaries, profile adversaries, CORAL, and GroupDRO did not significantly improve the held-out AUROC, either for a small CNN trained from scratch (gains above about $0.055$ are unlikely) or for heads on a frozen pretrained encoder (gains above about $0.04$ are unlikely). Two directions repeated across folds and families without reaching significance: GroupDRO (about $+0.02$) and averaging a CNN with a pretrained head. Acquisition statistics leak the label across sites and model scores follow them within class; however, the methods that reduce this did not improve accuracy, so we cannot say that the shortcut explains the accuracy loss. Seen-site validation is optimistic, standard conformal sets under-cover, and leave-site-out calibration avoids the worst shortfalls by paying in set size; this held for two encoder families and their ensemble. We suspect that invariance fails here because the data are tiny (sites of 52--207 images), the label-correlated signal is mostly within-site, and adversaries add optimization noise that small heads cannot afford; we did not test these explanations.

\subsection{Limitations}
The study covers one modality and one task. Our environment could not reach larger or credentialed datasets and had no GPU, and we used no private data. The collection is retrospective and assembled from publications and case reports, so absolute AUROC is not clinically meaningful, and ``Other'' and ``Unknown'' mix institutions. With eight sites, tiny test sets, and no multiplicity correction, ``no significant difference'' is not evidence of equivalence below roughly $0.03$ AUROC. The development/confirmation split was ours and not random, and tuning budgets were modest, so a better-tuned variant could still help. The pretrained encoder was frozen and not fine-tuned, and we did not check whether images in this benchmark also appear in its pretraining collections. The CNN confirmation results were known before the pretrained family was designed. No leave-site-out conformal rule has a finite-sample guarantee under site shift, and coverage is marginal over images, not conditional on class or patient. The profile estimators are proxies. Larger data, more sites, and fine-tuned encoders would be needed to show that invariance helps.

\section{Conclusion}
An invariance recipe built from class-conditional alignment and site-profile augmentation did not beat \erm{} on held-out sites under a protocol that froze settings before the confirmation run, and the same held for a frozen pretrained encoder. What lasted is a label-free leak diagnostic, a measured gap between seen-site validation and leave-site-out performance, the observation that standard conformal sets under-cover unseen sites in both encoder families, the finding that a site-fold CV+ rule avoids the worst cases at a cost in set size, and the finding that simulated acquisition shifts do not reproduce the real effect. Averaging a from-scratch CNN with a pretrained model is a cheap direction worth testing on more sites.
